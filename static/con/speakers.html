Formats & timing

Know your slot

01 / ~30 min

Conference talk

Target ~22–24 minutes of content + 6–8 minutes of live Q&A. We'll keep time and moderate questions for you.

02 / ~10 min

Lightning talk

One idea, tightly delivered. No live Q&A; questions move to the networking room.

03 / 30–40 min

Panel / fireside

A moderator guides the conversation. We'll align on questions with you beforehand.

Sessions are live by default. If you prefer, you may pre-record the talk and join live for Q&A — tell us during confirmation.

Slides & content

Make it useful, make it yours

  • 16:9 aspect ratio. Use the AenixCon slide template (sent on acceptance) or your own clean deck.
  • Lead with the takeaway. Tell people early what they'll be able to do afterward.
  • Big, legible text. Assume people watch on a laptop; small fonts and dense diagrams don't survive streaming.
  • Show real things. Config, code, dashboards, numbers — depth is why people came.
  • Community-first, not a pitch. It's fine to mention your product where relevant; a 30-minute ad is not.
  • Credit sources. Cite projects, papers and people; respect licenses on images and logos.
  • Have a demo backup. Record a short screencast in case a live demo misbehaves on the day.
  • Send slides 48h ahead. As a PDF backup, so we can keep the show running if your connection drops.

Online tech setup

The part that makes or breaks an online talk

Most problems at online events are audio and network, not content. Please treat this section as a checklist.

  • Wired internet if at all possible — or sit right next to the router. Have a phone hotspot as backup.
  • A headset or external mic. Laptop speakers cause echo; a $20 headset beats a $2,000 laptop mic.
  • Quiet room — mute notifications, close chat apps, silence the phone, tell people not to walk in.
  • Good, front-facing light. A window or lamp in front of you, not behind.
  • Use a laptop/desktop, not a phone or tablet, for screen-sharing.
  • Close heavy apps before you present; share a single window, not your whole desktop.
  • Attend the dry-run. We'll test your audio, screen-share and slides on the real platform.
  • Join early. Be in the green room 30 minutes before your slot; a host will be with you.

A member of the AenixCon team is in every room to run the timer, moderate Q&A, and jump in if anything breaks. You are never alone on stage.

Recording & consent

Everything is recorded

We record all sessions and publish them after the event. Ticket holders get the videos, and we may cut talks into shorter clips, quote you in write-ups, and feature slides. By speaking you grant AenixCon permission to record, edit and publish your session and materials, with attribution. If any part of your talk can't be recorded (e.g. a sensitive customer demo), tell us in advance and we'll pause the capture.

Ground rules

Code of Conduct

AenixCon is dedicated to a harassment-free, welcoming experience for everyone, regardless of gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion or technology choices.

  • Be respectful. Critique ideas, not people.
  • No harassment, discrimination or personal attacks — in talks, chat or networking rooms.
  • Keep content professional; no offensive imagery or language in slides or demos.
  • Respect privacy — don't share others' data or private conversations.

Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, contact the organizers immediately at conduct@aenix.io. Reports are handled confidentially. Organizers may warn, mute, remove or ban anyone violating this policy.

This Code of Conduct applies to all attendees, speakers, sponsors and organizers, across every session, chat and networking space.

Adapted in spirit from the CNCF Community Code of Conduct.
